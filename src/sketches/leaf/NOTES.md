# Leaf

One leaf (or one sprig, with its fruit) centred on the screen, drawn as a botanical study on warm paper. Finish layers three things, each on or off with its own settings beneath it: Outline (a plain 0.1 mm fineliner), Shading (hand hatching, line only) and Watercolour (a wash). Nothing moves on its own; changing a category morphs the shape. Raw WebGL2.

`shapes.js` builds the geometry (blade outline, lobes, margin, veins and veinlets, stalk, sprigs, fruit) at high resolution (`NMAX` 420 points a side, up to 40 segments a vein), since only one object is drawn. `buildShapes(P, B, variety)` builds the leaf variants and the sprig. It keeps its working state in module-level variables and typed arrays (`TX`/`TY`, `pl*` and `bladeMode` act as out-parameters) so a rebuild allocates nothing; the parts with no shared state of their own live in `knobs.js`, `layouts.js` and `util.js`.

## Files

| File | Role | Edit when |
|---|---|---|
| `params.js` | Every default: shape groups, then Finish (outline, shading and watercolour toggles with their settings, colours), plus species presets. | Any control |
| `shapes.js` | Geometry (see above): outline, veins, strokes, leaf variants, sprig, fruit, `buildShapes`. | Leaf, stem or fruit shape |
| `knobs.js` | Each select option as a row of numbers (`OUTLINE`, `BASE`, `APEX`, `LOBING`), resolved with the params into `G`, and jittered per variant into `J`. | A new option, or a new shape number |
| `layouts.js` | Where compound leaflets sit, for every leaf type, blended across types and counts. | Compound layouts |
| `util.js` | Clamp/lerp and the deterministic hashes and noise. | Rarely |
| `shaders.js` | Background pass and leaf pass. | The look |
| `sketch.js` | Morph weights, rebuild-on-change, fitting the object to the screen, GL. | New select, new buffer |
| `TODO.md` | Trees the engine can't draw yet (conifers, ginkgo, palms, thorns, flowers), with their looks, palettes and the features they need, and a roadmap of those features in build order. | Adding an engine feature |

## Species presets

`params.js` exports `presets`: each species is the current shape defaults (all groups but Finish, minus Scale and Morph time) plus its own overrides, so a species always lands on the same leaf. It also sets the colours (leaf, shade, stem, bud and fruit, and the paper), and leaves the rest of Finish (outline, shading, wash) alone. Most are in summer green; Aspen, Beech, Cherry and `Maple, Field` are in autumn colour (gold to scarlet, copper, orange to crimson, butter-yellow). Each paper is a very pale pastel from roughly opposite the leaf's hue (lilacs, roses, periwinkle under greens; blues and celadon under the autumn ones), or opposite a strong stem or leaf colour (apricot under the blue-grey of Willow and Eucalyptus, celadon under Dogwood's red twig). Names follow horticultural index convention: a qualified common name is inverted (`Oak, English`, `Maple, Field`), so relatives sit together in the menu; the `leaf-species` skill has the rules. There are 30, in alphabetical order; 17 bear fruit (see Fruit below), and `Apple, Crab` and `Oak, Holm` are there because of theirs. Node counts run 3–10 to suit the tree (big trees long sprigs, spur-leaved and opposite ones short), never 2, which looks unfinished. Hawthorn, Sweet gum, Tulip tree, Silk tree, Lilac and Hornbeam didn't read as themselves and aren't presets (see TODO.md for Hawthorn and Tulip tree). Whitebeam's silver underside is its shade colour set paler than its wash. Morphs between very different types (simple to compound, opposite to alternate) are smooth but busier; the cover sticks to similar trees. Colours glide during the morph, page colour included. The page cycles through every preset (manifest `autoplay`): it opens on a random one, then goes on in menu (alphabetical) order, wrapping round; it holds each 1 s, then morphs to the next, round and round, until the settings pane opens (a morph under way finishes, then it stops; closing the pane doesn't restart it, coming back to the page does). Nothing autoplay applies is saved (even when the pane opens mid-morph), so a user's saved settings (Finish, which presets don't touch) survive it; once you edit something, everything on screen is yours and is saved. Off for capture mode and prefers-reduced-motion. A preset morph takes 4 s (manifest `presetSeconds`); the cover (manifest `capture.presets`) holds each of `Oak, English`, Aspen and Eucalyptus for 1 s and morphs between them: all simple and alternate, so they morph into each other in one continuous blend, through green, autumn gold and scarlet, and blue-grey. The poster and link-preview image are the first frame, `Oak, English`. Everything morphs, including arrangement (see below).

## Smooth morphs and sway

- **Presets morph on one clock.** The sketch implements the kit's `morph(seconds)`, so a preset's numbers and selects land in P at once (the kit glides only the colours). `beginMorph` builds the final shape once to measure its box, then every eased number, every select weight and the framing move from where they are to where they end on one ease-in-out curve, so nothing starts abruptly or lands late, and the framing doesn't follow every wobble. A geometry change mid-morph (a slider) ends it, and the chase takes over from there.
- **Nothing passes through in-between layouts.** Every move must be monotonic, or leaves shiver in big morphs. So: (1) the leaflet count eases as pairs and terminal (`leafletPairs`, `leafletTerm` in PE), not as a raw count, because odd and even counts lay out differently; `blendLayouts` blends the four layouts around them, with slots keyed by identity (bipinnate side leaflets from a fixed slot, `PINNA_SIDE`). (2) The stem's zigzag kinks sit exactly on the nodes, each leaf taking the kink's bisector (kinks on a sample grid slid as the stem's length eased). (3) On lobed leaves, vein ends hand over smoothly between lobe tips instead of snapping to the nearest. To check a change, simulate a morph through shapes.js and count direction reversals of each leaf's on-screen centre: `Oak, English` → Elm, Elm → Ash and Ash → `Oak, English` should stay in single digits. Known: palmate venation still rounds the lobe count for its primary veins.
- **Two-phase morphs.** When the leaf type changes (simple ↔ compound, or between compound types), the old leaves shrink back into their nodes over the first 45% of the morph, the leaf's own shape (`LEAF_KEYS`: the Leaf, Lobes, Margin, Veins and Stalk groups) swaps at the midpoint while they're gone, and the new leaves grow out over the last 45%; the stem morphs throughout. Blending straight through turned the old leaves into odd half-compound ones. `leafGrow` scales every placement's presence.
- Shape numbers are eased copies (`PE` in sketch.js), so slider changes glide over Morph time. Counts go fractional while easing, and shapes.js grows the newest node, whorl leaf, leaflet pair (layouts blended either side of the count), vein or veinlet in as the fraction rises.
- Arrangement, Terminal and Bud type are eased weights like the other selects. The sprig is always built: "No stem" is a stem shrunk to nothing bearing only its terminal leaf (curve and zigzag scale with how much stem there is). Each placement carries a presence (`sprigPlace` slot 7); the renderer scales the leaf about where it attaches, so leaves grow out of and shrink into their nodes. Leaf shapes are keyed by node and position, not list index, so they don't swap as the arrangement changes. Alternate leaves take their side from node parity (pressed sprigs alternate), and Divergence only sets how near the stem they press, so nothing swings across the stem as it eases. Outline resolution steps only with the leaflet count rounded up to a power of two (and budgets for the maximum veins), because resampling the outline every frame while counts eased made the teeth shimmer.
- The bud is the end of the stem: drawn first, under every leaf. The tip runs past the last node by the bud's room, and leaves near the tip splay out so it shows.
- The framing eases toward the rebuilt bounds.
- Sway: the sprig rocks about its base and each leaf about its node, on two-sine rhythms with golden-angle phases, in scene time (pausing stops it). It redraws every frame while Sway > 0 (352 fps at 2560×1440 @2x on the M3); at 0 Leaf goes back to drawing only on change. Sway defaults to 0 (it added little), so the cover is still between morphs.

## Fruit

The Fruit group puts clusters of fruit on the sprig: in the axils of the lowest At nodes nodes, on the side away from the leaf, and optionally at the tip. Each cluster is a Cluster stalk ending in a fan (Spread °) of Per cluster fruit on their own stalks, Hang ° from the stem's direction. Fruit sit in sprig coordinates, so they're rebuilt with the stem, and are drawn last, over the leaves, in their own colours (Fruit, Fruit shade; an acorn's cup takes the bud colours). Gloss is a highlight patch reserved from the wash, as a watercolourist leaves paper bare: crisp-edged (its edge wanders by Halo jitter, like the wash's own), no pen line, growing with Gloss, in Highlight colour at Opacity (below 1 lets the fruit colour through). A soft gradient spot isn't something watercolour can do.

- **Types morph.** The Fruit select is eased weights like every other: the outline is the weighted blend of each type's half-width profile (`fruitW`), and each type's extras scale with its weight: a berry's crown, a drupe's seam, a key's veins, a nut's cup and its scales, a burr's spines. None's weight shrinks everything. At nodes, Per cluster and At tip ease too, growing the newest cluster or fruit out of its stalk.
- **Fruit length** stretches a fruit without widening it: shapes take a width scale apart from their length (`emitShape`'s `wid`), and fruit coordinates are u in lengths, w in widths (`fruitAt`).
- **Pen lines stop at the highlight.** A fruit's own lines (seam, key veins, berry crown) are drawn in fruit coordinates by `fruitLine`, which breaks them where `inHighlight` (the shader's highlight, mirrored on the CPU, plus a margin for its wandering edge) says paper is left bare. So Gloss, Opacity and Halo jitter rebuild geometry.
- **Keys pair outward.** Fruit fanned clockwise are mirrored (`emitShape`'s `mir`), so a pair of one-sided keys spread their wings away from each other. Key wing 0 gives the ash's symmetric strap.
- **Burr spines** are small filled cones (`emitShape` with 2 segments), drawn before the body so it covers their bases; pen hairlines vanished against the leaves.
- Buds, fruit, cups and spines all go through `emitShape` (a filled shape plus closed pen outline from a half-width profile). Fruit fills are `KIND_FRUIT` (8).

## How it's drawn

- **Stem wander:** Wander adds slow noise to the stem's heading, in arc length (leaf units), so nodes and leaves follow the drawn line and the wiggles keep their size as the stem grows; fruit and cluster stalks bow by the same amount.
- **Fit.** After every rebuild the object's box is measured (leaf vertices, or for a sprig the stem's vertices plus each placed leaf's box corners). It's rotated tip up and scaled so its larger side is Scale × 90% of the screen's short side, centred.
- **Paper** pass: the paper colour with a little tooth.
- **Per leaflet** (stencil): the wash fill marks the stencil; veins are clipped to it; then the pen outline on top. Sprig leaves draw tip first, so leaves nearer the base cover those above. Fruit (stalks, then each fruit whole) draw last, over everything.
- **Finish layers** (`u_penLines`, `u_shading`, `u_watercolour`, each 0 or 1), in any combination; with none, fills are bare paper (they still hide what's beneath). Outline is the pen: every outline, and pen edges down veins and stalks. Shading is hatching: solid lines at their own width (Hatch weight px), opacity and ink, mirrored across the midrib like veins, broken into short strokes that are each kept or dropped so tone comes from how many lines appear (no greys); a cross-hatch family joins in the darkest places; lines too dense to draw are dropped, not blended. Watercolour is the wash underneath the pen work; without Outline it's a pen-less painting (the halo still leaves its sliver of paper).
- **Wash:** transparent pigment over paper: one half in the shade colour, bleeding unevenly across the midrib; blooms; granulation from the paper grain (one grain texture, Grain + Grain scale, shared with the paper); a dried rim. Its edge is set by Paper halo ± Halo jitter as a share of each spoke (midrib → margin). Fill vertices carry their spoke length and direction, so for a negative halo the margin vertices are pushed out and the wash overshoots the pen line.
- **Veins:** pass 1 (watercolour) pools pigment along their sides; pass 2 clears hatching and wash from them and draws the pen along both edges (one line down veins too thin for two).
- **Pen:** one fine sepia line width for everything (Finest line px), with occasional skips (Pen skip).

## Gotchas

- **Fill anchors.** The fill is quads from an anchor on the midrib to each outline point. Palmate lobes push the outline along rays from the palm point, so a deep sinus doubles back as seen from the midrib, and the quads filled the notch (`Maple, Norway` showed it worst). With palmate lobing the anchors move to the palm point, from which the outline never doubles back.
- **Leaf base vs stalk.** Every stalked simple blade's base flares to at least 1.8× the midrib's half-width (the winged-petiole flare, `wingJoin`), so a wedge base doesn't taper to nothing where it meets a wider stalk (Magnolia). Leaflets do the same for their own stalks (in blade units, so divided by the leaflet's scale), and leaflet stalks start exactly where the petiole or rachis they grow from is, as wide as it is there, tapering into the midrib (stubs started short of the petiole's end leave gaps and a star of planks at the joint). Truncate's base exponent is 0.35: lower draws ruler-straight bottoms. Stipules are drawn after the stalks, each whole (fill, midrib, outline) like a leaflet, so they sit over the stalk and the second covers the first where they overlap; staged with the stalks, their fills went under the stalk and their outlines over it.

- **Vertex budget.** Each leaf variant gets an equal share of the buffer (one share kept for the sprig). Mid-morph between two compound types, both layouts' leaflets are out at once and a variant can need more than its share (bipinnate to anything is the worst). `buildShapes` rebuilds such a variant at half, then a quarter of the resolution, then cuts it at its share; otherwise the buffer overflows, cutting off the stem and the last leaves, and an empty leaf's box (±1e9) throws the framing out. Every preset at rest uses under half its share, so none is ever rebuilt. A leaf with no vertices adds nothing to the framing box. Fruit go into the `sFill` stage, sized for the most the sliders allow (10 nodes and the tip, 5 burrs each with 40 spines: 115k vertices); fruit are appended last, so if a huge compound leaf ever leaves no room, it's the fruit that are cut off.

- Shader sources are JS template strings: a backtick in a GLSL comment ends the string and breaks the build.
- A blade's lines go to separate staging buffers (veinlets, veins, midrib, outline) and are appended in draw order. Reordering vertices in place afterwards was the biggest cost in a rebuild.

## Performance budget

- **Drawn on demand.** The leaf is still, so `frame()` returns without drawing when nothing changed (no param edit, morph, rebuild or resize); WebGL keeps the last image on screen. Redrawing a full-screen capillary network every frame kept the GPU busy, and Chrome's own UI shares that GPU, so the whole tab and the mouse stuttered. Consequence: `bench.mjs` fps for this sketch measures the idle loop, not drawing. Measure drawing by driving changes (e.g. dragging a slider) instead.
- **Uploads rotate through a ring of two fixed-size buffers** (`RING` in sketch.js), each with its own VAO, written with `bufferSubData`. Writing into a buffer the GPU is still drawing from makes the driver wait (one upload stalled 3.8 s), and reallocating with `bufferData` each time stalled too. Each buffer gets one full-size upload at start, so the driver sizes its staging for the largest geometry up front.
- Memory is flat across hundreds of rebuilds (JS heap 1–2 MB); nothing is held between them.

- The wash is a handful of texture lookups per pixel (the old light-table capillary network was two Voronoi lookups and cost far more). Stress test (drags and flips, 2×): worst frame 48 ms for a whorled sprig drag, ~2–3 ms for simple-leaf drags.
- Geometry rebuilds only on shape changes; at this resolution a rebuild is a few ms for a simple leaf, more for compound leaves and sprigs (Node, M3: `Oak, English` 4.7 ms, Elm 5.7 ms, Ash 6.9 ms). Fruit add about 0.3 ms at most to the presets that bear it (`Oak, English` 4.5 → 4.8 ms with acorns); the most fruit the sliders allow (55 spiny burrs) builds in about 10 ms. A preset morph builds once more at its start, to measure where it ends.
