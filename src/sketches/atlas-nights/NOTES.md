# Atlas Nights

A night in the High Atlas, facing south, looped in about a minute and a half. Month sets the season; the default is early June, so the summer sky (Scorpius, Sagittarius) rules the night. A short twilight with no sunrise, then the real sky turns overhead: the stars come out brightest first, constellations trace themselves in as they rise and fade once they pass due south, and now and then a shooting star falls. Film grain over everything. Raw WebGL2.

References: the user's photos in `~/repos/albums/src/albums/2025-sunbirds/`: `media/IMG_1171.jpg` (the twilight colours and the ridge; the bar is how vivid that sunset was) and `b-roll/IMG_5863.jpg` (a sky full of stars).

| `label.js` | The hovered constellation's name: a DOM lozenge over the canvas. | The label's look |

| `check.mjs` | Params ↔ GLSL ↔ `perFrame()`, `hover()` and `label.js` (via `scripts/lib/shader-check.mjs`). | Rarely |

## How it works

- **One day per loop, as a pure function of scene time.** Phase 0 is the brightest twilight (the sun due south, just below the horizon) and ±0.5 is the dead of night. The sidereal time is the sun's RA (from Month) plus its hour angle, so the sky turns once per loop in step with the sun, and the loop is seamless. One loop shows far more sky than a real night would: nearly every constellation that rises at this latitude passes through. Nothing is stored between frames: Speed, pause, captures and param edits can't leave anything half-drawn.
- **Twilight** (`tw`, 0–1) is a smooth bump of width Twilight share around phase 0. The sun's azimuth runs south → west through dusk, so the evening glow drifts right as it fades and the morning glow comes up on the left.
- **Projection:** a pinhole (gnomonic) camera, like the photo's lens, so the horizon and every constellation line come out straight. Field of view is top to bottom, but at least Min width across (phones in portrait zoom out). The southern horizon is pinned at Horizon height. `perFrame()` builds one matrix, equatorial unit vector → camera space (`f_eq2cam`); the composite pass inverts the projection per pixel to get altitude and azimuth.
- **Sky** (composite): night colour with airglow low down, under the twilight gradient: warm toward the sun, cool away from it (mixed by azimuth from the sun, Glow width). Each side is six evenly spaced stops (horizon, low, middle, high, deep, night) joined by a smooth Hermite curve that arrives at night flat; straight blends between stops left visible bands. The stops were sampled from IMG_1171. The gradient's height is measured as the altitude straight up the middle of the screen at each row, so its bands lie level as in the photo (true altitude curves up toward the edges of a wide view and bowled it); stars, lines and the ridge use true altitude. Midpoint places the ramp's halfway colour (Schlick's bias, smooth, no kink): below 0.5 it packs the warm colours toward the horizon and stretches the blue-blacks above. **It sets rather than fades:** Glow height (0–1) is its length at the peak, with 1 = its top at the top edge of the view; through the twilight its top slides from there down to the horizon (`f_glow`, from `tw`).
- **Stars** (points, additive into a half-float light target): magnitude plus extinction (more air near the horizon) against the faintest magnitude the twilight allows, so the brightest appear first. Brightness is `flux^contrast`, because true flux would hide almost every star. Colour comes from B-V (Ballesteros temperature → blackbody fit). Twinkle runs on real time and is stronger through more air. Star cores are at least ~¾ canvas px wide; narrower ones lose most of their light between pixel centres.
- **Mask** (Swirl group): on, the swirl shows only through the lines (below). Off, it replaces the sky, behind the stars, light leaks and ridge, and the lines draw nothing (their mask is multiplied by it); hovering and the flares still work.
- **Constellation lines are a mask, not a colour.** The lines pass writes how much line covers each pixel into the light target's alpha (stars and meteors write colour into rgb and leave alpha alone); the composite pass shows the swirl through that mask. A hovered line is partly white instead (its Lighten share goes to rgb), so it lifts toward white over the swirl.
- **Swirl** (fields pass, ¼ resolution, **on the sky**): each pixel's direction is turned back into an equatorial vector (the transpose of `f_eq2cam`) and the noise is taken there, so the swirl turns with the stars and the lines don't slide through it; only its own churn (Speed) moves it against them. Domain-warped fbm (a 3D warp), drifting through the noise on scene time, mapped through the same twilight ramps as the sky (horizon to high only, never deep or night), warm or cool by region with a narrow blend (averaging the two ramps gives grey), then saturated. Scale counts swirls per screen height near the centre.
- **Light leaks flare off the lines' edges, like solar flares** (flare pass, ½ resolution, as its edges are crisp; the same segment instances and geometry as the lines via `LINE_SEGMENT`). Each *side* of each segment is cut into sections Section long, and each section flickers in time slots on real time, on with chance Share, so about that share of each edge is flaring; the two sides choose separately, so flares aren't mirrored. A flare's outline is two octaves of simplex noise in line space (along, away), stretched outward by Stretch and streaming outward at Rise; the tongue reaches env × noise of Reach, so its edge is ragged (Softness sets how crisp). A hovered constellation blends to its own full set (Leaks, reach, share, section, detail, stretch, rise, softness, in the Hover group; its sections default long, so it flares in unbroken runs) and holds its sections steady. The composite adds swirl × (1 − e^−flare): big flares saturate toward the swirl's own colours rather than clipping to white. Seed picks which sections flare.
- **Sky cultures** (the Sky culture dropdown, `cultures.js`): western, d3-celestial's 88 drawn by rank (main / more / all 88); and three Arabic traditions with a history in Morocco, from Stellarium's sky cultures by Khalid al-Ajaji: the lunar mansions (manāzil), al-Ṣūfī's 10th-century Book of the Fixed Stars, and the older Bedouin asterisms. **Those three are CC BY-ND 4.0: no derivatives.** `sky-data.mjs` includes every constellation and line unaltered, only converting Hipparcos numbers to coordinates (from d3-celestial's stars, else VizieR's Hipparcos catalogue); don't trim, merge or redraw them. Each segment carries its culture; the line and flare shaders and `hover()` skip all but the chosen one (`f_culture`). The label shows a constellation's name, then its native name where the culture gives one. **Amazigh: Tuareg** is the sky Henri Duveyrier recorded from the Kel Ajjer in *Les Touareg du Nord* (1864, pp. 423–426; checked against the page scans): his names, meanings and stars, our lines (`scripts/sky-tuareg.mjs`; single named stars are zero-length segments, hoverable, not drawn). The stories behind the labels: Amanâr ("he who opens", Orion) is a hunter girded with his belt, followed by his dog Eydi (Sirius) and preceded by the gazelles Ihenkâdh (Lepus); in another telling he climbs out of a muddy well and Adâr-n-elâkou, "the foot in the mud" (Rigel), is the last star to rise, after the jujube fruit has fallen. Tâlemt, the she-camel (Ursa Major), is milked while Lemkechen ("hold!", Polaris), a woman told to hold the calf Aourâ (Ursa Minor), stays frozen with fear of El-Djema'at, the assembly (ψ λ μ ν ξ UMa) plotting to kill her. Chêt-Ahadh, the daughters of the night (the Pleiades), are six named sisters and a boy's eye that flew to the sky. Tâzzeït, the palm (Scorpius), has the young man Amrôt (Antares) stopped halfway up it to watch girls in red come from the pool. Ifarakfarâken (ε δ η CMa) is the flutter of a fan: its rising brings violent winds. Tenâfelit and Tôzzert, wealth and poverty (δ and ο Velorum, low on the horizon), mark due south for crossing the Tanezrouft. Moroccan Amazigh (Tashelhit) star lore exists in Laoust's *Mots et choses berbères* (1920, "Les étoiles"), but untranslated; it's not drawn here.
- **Constellation lines** (instanced quads): each segment carries its constellation's centre, rank and place in drawing order. Tracing in follows the centre's hour angle (Start drawing → Fully drawn), fading follows it too (Start fading → Gone). At dusk tracing waits for the twilight to drop below Dark enough; at dawn lines fade as it passes it. So everything already up traces in together as night falls, then each constellation draws itself as it rises.
- **Hover and touch** (`hover.js`, `input.js`, `label.js`): the canvas has its own crosshair cursor (a plain 21px cross in Text colour at Cursor opacity, PNGs at 1× and 2× in an `image-set`, redrawn only when those params change); the pane and buttons keep their own. A constellation is picked when the pointer is within Reach of a showing line (the same hour-angle and twilight logic as `LINE_SEGMENT`, repeated on the CPU: keep them in step), or inside one of its enclosed shapes, so the hover holds while a ring of stars drifts past a still pointer. The shapes are found once at load (`buildLoops`): each constellation's lines as a graph, one loop per edge outside a spanning tree, which between them cover every enclosed space; a shape counts once its constellation is traced in. The pick eases into `f_hover` over Fade in, and the one before falls over Fade out (2s); the name's CSS fade matches. **Touch:** a tap picks the same way, holds for Tap hold, then fades, and its name rides on the tapped sky point (one transform per frame while it shows). **Drag:** a press that moves more across than up or down scrubs scene time (right = forward, a screen width = Drag span of a loop); more vertical than horizontal is ignored entirely. Names: a native name first, upright and bidi-isolated, then the translation in brackets.
- **Shooting stars:** time is cut into slots three meteor-durations long, each holding one with a hashed chance, moment and place (`kit/random.js` `hash2`), so the loop averages Per loop. Trails point away from the Geminid radiant when it's up (December's shower; in other months it's often down, and trails slant at random). Seed picks a different run.
- **Ridge:** a 512-sample height profile from the photo, stored as a share of the photo's width, so Span sets both its width and its height in degrees. Mirrored beyond the traced span.

**Composite order:** sky → light (stars, meteors, and the swirl through the line mask) → light leaks (flare × swirl) → ridge → vignette → exposure → dust → grain.

## Performance

- The light pass draws ~8,300 points, ~700 line quads and 2 meteor quads; the vertex shaders cull anything below the horizon, behind the camera or too faint, before it costs fill.
- The fields pass (¼ resolution) holds all the noise: 4 fbm3 + 1 snoise (13 simplex calls) per pixel, about Blackstar's fields pass. Put any new smooth noise here.
- The flare pass (½ resolution) draws ~700 quads as wide as the larger Reach each side; each fragment is 2 snoise and a few hashes. Lower its resolution (`flareDivisor` in shader.js) and the ragged edges turn blocky.
- The composite pass is one full-screen pass with no noise calls: an inverse projection, two colour ramps, two texel fetches, one ridge lookup, then the shared film layer (~7 hash chains, as in Blackstar).
- `sky.js` is ~164KB (105KB gzipped): 8,297 stars, and 2,450 segments across the four sky cultures.
